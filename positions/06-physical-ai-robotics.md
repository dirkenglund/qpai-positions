# Physical AI for Scientific Discovery

## Overview

**Lead:** Prof. Dirk Englund englund@mit.edu (Principal Investigator)

We are building a next-generation autonomous laboratory for scientific discovery in quantum optics and photonics. This position targets researchers at the intersection of AI, robotics, and physical sciences who want to develop intelligent systems that interact with and reason about the physical world.

## Vision: Closing the Loop Between AI and the Physical World

Traditional AI models learn from static datasets. Our Physical AI initiative creates systems that:

- **Perceive** the physical state of optical experiments through sensors and computer vision
- **Reason** about quantum optical phenomena using physics-informed neural networks
- **Act** on the physical world through robotic manipulation of optical components
- **Learn** from experimental outcomes to refine both models and experimental strategies

## Laboratory Infrastructure

### Robotic Platform
- **7 Franka Research 3 robotic arms** configured for collaborative manipulation on a precision optical table
- High-resolution force/torque sensing for delicate component handling
- Sub-millimeter positioning accuracy for optical alignment tasks
- Real-time control at 1kHz with ROS 2 integration

### Computational Brain
- **500+ GB aggregate GPU memory** (NVIDIA H100 cluster)
- Hybrid cloud-edge architecture for real-time inference and training
- Support for large reasoning models (70B+ parameter LLMs)
- Integrated experiment orchestration and data management

### Quantum Optics Testbed
- Free-space and fiber-coupled optical setups
- Single-photon sources and detectors
- Programmable photonic processors
- Integrated photonic chip characterization stations

## Research Directions

### 1. Robotic Optical Alignment
Develop vision-guided manipulation algorithms that can:
- Automatically align free-space optical components
- Fiber couple and optimize coupling efficiency
- Handle and characterize photonic integrated circuits

### 2. Physics-Informed Robot Learning
Create models that understand optical physics:
- Learn from Maxwell's equations and quantum optics theory
- Generate physically plausible manipulation strategies
- Predict experimental outcomes before execution

### 3. Reasoning Models for Experiment Design
Build AI systems that can:
- Propose novel experimental configurations
- Explain quantum optical phenomena
- Generate hypotheses and design experiments to test them

### 4. Digital Twin Integration
Connect robotic systems to our [digital twin framework](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41566-022-01086-9):
- Real-time synchronization between physical and simulated experiments
- Transfer learning between simulation and reality
- Automated calibration and model refinement
