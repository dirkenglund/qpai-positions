# Solid State Atoms

﻿Theorist and Experimentalist Positions in Solid-State Spin Systems for Quantum Networks and Quantum Sensing 
We are now poised for the development of a quantum internet to exchange quantum information and distribute entanglement among quantum memories (and ultimately quantum computers) that could be great distances apart. This kind of quantum internet would have a range of applications that aren't possible in a classical world, including long-distance, unconditionally-secure communication, precision sensing and navigation, and distributed quantum computing. But we still need to develop and perfect the types of components and protocols to build such a quantum internet. With our collaborators, the MIT Quantum Photonics group is developing these quantum devices and systems, focusing on photonic integrated circuits, spin-based quantum memories, and prototype networks [1–5].
Project descriptions: 
1. Quantum Networks: Communications and Computing, theory and experiments.

The project focuses on theory and experiments of quantum communications and quantum computation, with a particular emphasis on:
* Experimental quantum repeater development and deployment in our Boston-area quantum internet testbed [1,2,5–8] in a collaboration with researchers at Harvard and MIT Lincoln Laboratory.  
* Theory on quantum network protocols and blueprints [3,4,9–13]
   * First-principles theory of distributed quantum sensing on quantum networks; quantum sensors driven for distributed quantum-enhanced neural network inference (see ML papers[14–16]; quasi-on-demand entanglement sources[17]
* Experimental investigation of novel quantum memories, with a focus on:
   * Color centers in diamond (quantum control of SiV, SnV, robotic big-data & AI-driven spectroscopy, ..) and their integration with photonic circuits, see here [5,7,8,18–27]
2. Quantum Sensing and Metrology
  
  
  
  

Solid-state quantum systems are emerging as excellent sensors. Spins in diamond, in particular, form the basis of a new generation of magnetometers, thermometers, electric field sensors, inertial sensors, etc[28–31][32]. In particular, we are focusing on strongly coupling diamond spin ensembles to microwave resonators, which has enabled leading magnetometers with sub-pT sensitivities[33] as well as led to new discoveries such as microwave mode cooling[34].
Project description: With scientist Dr. Matt Trusheim (mtrush@mit.edu) of MIT RLE and the Army Research Laboratory (experiments) and with long-time collaborator Prof Kurt Jacobs of UMD and Army Research Laboratory (AMO theory, open systems quantum theory) as well as long-time collaborator Dr. Danielle Braje of Lincoln Laboratory, we are pushing these sensors to new levels of performance through a combination of theory, modeling, material processing, and system integration. The project will push the state of the art in these devices and explore new avenues and applications, applying aspects from machine learning, sensing protocol design, atomic-molecular optical (AMO) physics, electrical engineering, and computer science to develop world-leading experimental and applied quantum sensing platforms.
