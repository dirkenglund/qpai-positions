# Research Engineer Nanofab

﻿Research Engineer in Nanofabrication of Quantum Materials & Devices
Cambridge, Massachusetts, United States   | Posting date: XXXX 2024
A leading quantum information processing platform consists of diamond color centers, which have enabled demonstrations of deterministic remote entanglement and large-scale hybrid integration into photonic integrated circuits (PIC). The MIT Quantum Photonics group has pioneered diamond nanofabrication[1–3] and on-chip heterogeneous integration[4], working  across the full-stack of diamond technology to enable modular solid-state spin qubits for scalable quantum network nodes and computing. Together with our partners MITRE Corporation and Sandia National Laboratory in the Quantum Moonshot Program, we are currently looking for exceptional talent to join us in building the “quantum internet”[5].
  

Fig.1: Join our team at MIT’s brand new fabrication facility, MIT.nano (left), to create the materials and devices underpinning next-generation quantum technologies such as spin-photon gates (right).
Job summary:
The diamond process engineer will streamline and systematically improve nanofabrication of diamond quantum devices (building on recently developed processes[1–3]).
Responsibilities:
* Conduct creation of defect centers in diamond. This includes polishing via chemical etching, coordinating with external vendors for ion implantations, tri-acid cleaning, high-temperature annealing. See Fig.2.
* Conduct the diamond fabrication to realize diamond devices at MIT.nano, a state of the art cleanroom centrally located in MIT’s campus in Cambridge, MA. Responsibilities include lithography (Electron Beam Lithography, Photolithography), thin-film technology (Chemical Vapor Deposition, Atomic Layer Deposition, Electron Beam Evaporation), etching (Dry & Wet Etching).
* Conduct hybrid integration of fabricated diamond devices into PICs or CMOS chips.
Experience/Qualifications:
* Experience in cleanroom fabrication on lithography, thin-film technology, and etching process.
* A passion for tinkering, hacking, innovation, and changing the world for the better.
* Bachelor’s degree is preferred in a technical field (EE, physics, material science, etc), though deep experience in R&D and hands-on work is more important than any degree.
